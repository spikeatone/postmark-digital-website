§1What just changed

A cruise company and a resort company now share one guest

On September 23, Royal Caribbean Group agreed to take a 50 percent stake in Sandals and Beaches Resorts, for about $3 billion. A joint board led by Adam Stewart and Jason Liberty will govern the partnership, and the deal is expected to close in early 2027.

On one side: Royal Caribbean, Celebrity Cruises, and Silversea, private destinations like Perfect Day CocoCay and the Royal Beach Clubs, a new river cruise line, and Points Choice, which already lets a guest move loyalty points between Crown & Anchor Society, Captain's Club, and the Venetian Society. On the other: more than forty years of Caribbean all-inclusive hospitality, adults-only Sandals and family Beaches across the islands, Island Routes, and Sandals Select.

Read what both companies said that week, and three commitments stand out:

  1. Sandals stays Sandals. Beaches stays Beaches. The resorts will not become beach clubs for cruise passengers, and the point is not to sell cruisers day passes.
  2. Recognition, both ways. A Sandals guest on a Royal Caribbean ship should be recognized for their status, and a cruise guest at Sandals the same. Jason Liberty was candid that getting there will take new technology.
  3. A whole life of travel. The ambition, in Liberty's words, is to give guests "a lifetime of vacations," on water or on land.
Our read

The deal puts two of the most loyal customer bases in travel under one roof. What it doesn't have yet is the layer that knows one guest across both: who they are, what they loved, where they are in their trip, and what should come next. That layer is what this proposal is about, and the first step is a pair of working prototypes that show how it could work.

Deal details are from the companies' joint announcement of September 23, 2026, and executive interviews and analyst notes published that week.

§2The idea

One guest, one profile, every brand

The app rests on two engines: a Guest Profile (who the guest is) and a Journey State (where they are in the trip). Every screen, every message, and the navigation itself are drawn from those two things. Put the same engines under a company that owns ships, private islands, and all-inclusive resorts, and they stop describing one trip. They describe a life of them.

For Sandals, it means a honeymoon couple who arrive already known, not two names on a rooming list. For Royal Caribbean, it means eight hours in port built backwards from the all-aboard time around what each guest actually likes. For both, it means the couple who honeymooned at Sandals stepping aboard a Royal Caribbean ship as recognized guests, and the cruise guests who fell for an island coming back to a Sandals for a week.

That is the difference between two loyalty programs that have been connected and one company that actually knows its guest.

The guest appFor the guest: plans, books, and reshapes itself around the trip, on water and on land.
Guest ConnectFor every team on every ship and at every resort: who this guest is, right now.
The partner portalFor the islands' operators: the approved map of everything beyond the gate.

All three read from one guest profile. The guest app is how the partnership learns what a guest wants. Guest Connect is how every team acts on it. The partner portal is how the islands take part.

Design rule: the guest owns the profile. Nothing one brand learns reaches another without the guest's yes, and every piece of recognition can be seen and switched off. Recognition people choose feels like hospitality. Recognition they didn't choose feels like surveillance.

§3What each side gets

A new stream of guests on each side, and one relationship across both

On land · Sandals and Beaches

Guests who met the islands from a ship and want to come back for a week. Cruise status recognized at check-in. And a better way to fill shoulder weeks: analysts noted that more than a dozen Sandals resorts sit within a half hour of ports Royal Caribbean calls at. The app can offer a quiet week, or two nights before a sailing, to exactly the guests whose profile fits, instead of discounting to everyone.

On water · Royal Caribbean Group

Couples and families who trust Sandals and have never considered a cruise, introduced by a brand they already love, at the moment their life says they're ready. Port days that feel personal instead of generic. And status that follows the guest ashore, so the relationship doesn't end at the gangway.

What both get

  1. Recognition that is real. Status, preferences, allergies, and the occasion being celebrated, known on every ship and at every resort from the moment the guest arrives, and in the hands of the team member standing in front of them. This is the technology the partnership said it would need.
  2. The right next vacation, not the next available one. The profile knows the life stage and the taste. A couple gets Sandals. A young family gets Beaches, or three nights to Perfect Day CocoCay. The big birthday gets Silversea. Cross-selling that feels like advice from someone who knows you.
  3. Travel advisors at the center. Advisors sell a great deal of both companies' vacations. With the guest's consent, the advisor sees what the app knows and gets credit for every booking the app inspires. The app should make an advisor better, never route around one.
  4. One view of the guest, for one board. What guests want, what they actually do, and what they loved, on ship and on shore. Insight that helps decide which ports matter, where the next Beaches goes, and what the next private destination should feel like.

How we'd measure it

Not downloads. The measures that matter to this partnership: guests who have taken both a ship and a resort vacation, time between vacations, repeat rate within and across brands, advisor-attributed bookings, and guest satisfaction where recognition was used against where it wasn't. We'd agree the targets together before launch.

§4The engines, extended

Two engines, built for water and land

Together the engines give a one-minute interview, a profile with a confidence score on every dimension, seven journey states inferred on their own, and a feed, a trip, and a Today view that reshape around them. Here is what they become when they span every ship and every resort in the partnership.

4.1 Guest Profile: one guest, many brands

The profile starts with a one-minute interview at the first booking and sharpens with every trip. Across the partnership it gets richer, faster, because a week at sea or at a resort says a great deal about a person. All of it with consent.

One set of axes for everything the partnership sells

The same seven taste axes that score the Friday night jump-up in Gros Islet score a resort, a ship, an itinerary, and a shore excursion. That is what lets the app compare a Beaches week with a family sailing honestly, and explain the choice in one line.

One honeymoon couple. The same seven axes score every resort, ship, sailing, and shore experience, so the app can match them to any of them.

Accurate, not presumptuous

Every dimension carries a confidence score. Where the app is unsure, it asks. Where it is sure, it acts. Across brands this matters more, not less: nothing is more off-putting than a stranger who knows too much. The prototype already shows the guest exactly what it believes, and how sure it is.

4.2 Journey State: many trips, one guest

The core has seven states, from Discovering to Between Trips. Across ships and resorts the trip itself gains three modes, and a single trip can chain them: two nights at a resort, then a week at sea with a port every other day.

Between Trips is where the partnership pays off. For a resort company alone, the quiet months after a trip are a wait for a repeat booking. For a company with ships and resorts, they are the moment to suggest the right next vacation, whichever kind it is.

New dials

DialStarting valueWhat it controls
Port day buffer60 minHow early the app gets a guest back to the ship before all-aboard

Design rule: the all-aboard time is a hard constraint, like a booking. On a port day the app plans backwards from the ship. It never suggests a plan that makes a guest run for the gangway.

§5Dream with us

Ten years of one guest, as Sandals and Royal Caribbean see it

Follow one couple, Maya and Jordan, for ten years, told the way the partnership would see it: what each brand knows, what it does with it, and what it's worth. The couple is invented. Everything that happens uses a piece of the app described here.

2027 · Sandals · Saint Lucia

Sandals books a honeymoon, and meets the guest

A travel advisor books a honeymoon at a Sandals in Saint Lucia. Today, Sandals learns two names, the dates, and a room category. With the app, the confirmation carries an invitation, and one minute later Sandals knows far more: this couple leans quiet and slow, would rather have a private beach than a swim-up bar, and one of them doesn't eat shellfish.

On property, the front desk, the dining room, and the butler open the couple's card in Guest Connect and see what the couple chose to share, so the first dinner arrives without shellfish and nobody has to ask twice. A bartender notes, in two taps, that Jordan takes still water with no ice. When rain rolls in on day four, the app rebuilds their day around the spa and a cooking class, and keeps the catamaran they booked, because a booking is a fact, not a suggestion. Island Routes sees demand for a quiet rainforest morning before the couple has thought to ask.

What it's worth to Sandals: a guest profile from the very first booking, a honeymoon that feels personal from the first hour, and an advisor credited for everything the app inspires.

2029 · Sandals, then Royal Caribbean · Barbados

Royal Caribbean wins a guest who had never considered a cruise

Two years on, the couple starts browsing dates again. On its own, Royal Caribbean could not have reached them: they have never cruised, and never searched for one. The partnership can. Their profile says autumn, a week, somewhere new, and quiet over crowds, so the app offers two nights at a Sandals in Barbados, then seven nights on a Royal Caribbean ship sailing the southern Caribbean from Bridgetown. One trip, two brands, one itinerary.

At the gangway, the crew member scanning their SeaPass cards sees their Sandals status in Guest Connect and welcomes them by name. The dining room knows about the shellfish allergy and the still water, because the guests said it could. The stateroom attendant sees that Sandals Barbados already did the anniversary cake two days ago, and plans something else. In every port the app plans backwards from the all-aboard time.

What it's worth: to Royal Caribbean, a first-time cruiser introduced by a brand they already trust, greeted like a regular on day one. To Sandals, two nights sold that would not otherwise exist.

2031 · Beaches and Royal Caribbean

Beaches and Perfect Day CocoCay keep the family years

When the couple adds a toddler to their party, adults-only Sandals is no longer the answer. A company with one brand would lose them here. This one doesn't. The app steps Sandals back and brings Beaches Turks and Caicos and a three-night sailing to Perfect Day CocoCay forward, each with a reason the family can see: "Because you'd rather have shallow water than a waterpark line, and you still like a slow pace." Life stage decides what they see, not which brand has rooms to fill.

What it's worth: a family that stays inside the portfolio through the years when most couples drift to someone else.

2035 · Silversea

Silversea meets a guest it already understands

For a fortieth birthday, the profile, eight years deep, says small crowds, deep culture, no rush. The app suggests Silversea for the first time, and says why in one line. Silversea's first conversation with this guest starts where the last brand's ended.

What it's worth to Silversea: a new luxury guest, acquired by a reason line instead of an ad budget.

2037 · Sandals · Saint Lucia

Sandals gets them back for ten years

"Ten years ago this week, you were in Saint Lucia." The app says it once, in the right week, and offers the resort where it all began. At check-in the front desk already has their card open in Guest Connect: status earned on ships and at resorts, the room they like, the table they always ask for, and a note from the Silversea crew about the wine they loved. The team greets them like old friends, because to the company, that is what they are. Sandals never had to ask twice.

What it's worth: the most valuable guest in travel. Five vacations across four brands, and the sixth already taking shape with you.

And from the ship's side

A Royal Caribbean couple on a first southern Caribbean sailing has eight hours in Saint Lucia. Royal Caribbean knows they lean toward adventure and local food, so the app builds a rainforest morning and lunch in town, and gets them back on board with an hour to spare. Nobody sends them to a resort pool for the afternoon. Three weeks later, Sandals sees a couple whose profile says they loved the island, and offers them a week there.

The port day was the trailer. The week at Sandals is the film.

Every brand keeps its face

A Beaches trip wears Beaches' look and voice. A Silversea voyage wears Silversea's. The guest never feels handed from one company to another, and no brand has to become another brand's feature. The engines underneath are shared; the faces are each brand's own. That is how Sandals stays Sandals in software.

Principles that hold on every ship and at every resort

  • Every recommendation explains itself, and tapping the reason lets the guest correct the profile.
  • States are sticky, transitions are gentle. When the mode changes, the app says so once, warmly, and offers the new mode. It never rearranges itself silently.
  • Never an empty screen. Give dates, get a whole trip back. Editing a draft beats starting from nothing.
  • Useful without the profile. Refused permissions, a ship with no signal, a guest who never did the interview: every screen still works.
§7Guest Connect (working name)

Every team greets every guest like an old friend

The guest app is half the ecosystem. The other half is in the hands of the people who make the vacation: front desks and gangways, dining rooms and bars, butlers and stateroom attendants, the spa, the dive shop, guest services. Guest Connect gives every one of them what the partnership knows about the guest in front of them, at the moment it's useful, so a first meeting feels like a return.

7.1 The right detail, for the right person, at the right moment

TeamSeesDoesn't need
Front desk and gangwayNames and how to say them, status across every brand, the occasion, arrival notesDining notes, spa bookings
Dining rooms and barsAllergies and dietary needs, always; seating and drink preferences; the occasionRoom preferences, past complaints
Butlers and stateroom attendantsEverything the guest shared for this stay, and what other brands already did for the occasionNotes the guest has hidden
Spa, excursions, activitiesPace, intensity, mobility, what they loved last timeDining and room notes
Guest servicesEverything shared, plus anything that went wrong on a past trip, so it can be made rightOnly what the guest has hidden
  • Today's arrivals, before they arrive. Every morning each team gets its list: who is checking in or boarding, what matters about each guest, and what's being celebrated.
  • A card in two taps. By name, room, or stateroom, or when a guest checks in, scans a SeaPass card, or books a table. No cameras, no facial recognition.
  • Handoffs between brands. When a guest leaves a Sandals and boards a ship two days later, the crew knows where they've come from and what has already been done, so no gesture is repeated and no preference is lost.

7.2 Teams add what they learn

A bartender notices a guest takes still water with no ice. A butler learns that one of them is nervous on boats. Two taps, and it's on the card for the next team, the next ship, and the next resort. Every note shows up in the guest's own app, under what our teams remember about you, where the guest can correct it or delete it. There are no secret files.

7.3 Built for the floor, not the back office

  • On a phone, one-handed, between courses or at the gangway. Arrivals are cached, so it works on ship Wi-Fi and when the signal drops.
  • In the team member's own language. Crews come from all over the world; the card reads in theirs.
  • On top of the systems both companies already run. Guest Connect reads from reservations, loyalty, and property and ship systems. It doesn't replace them.

Design rule: teams see what the guest shared, and the guest can see what teams see. Access follows the role, every view is logged, and nothing sensitive is ever inferred. Allergies and declared medical needs reach every team that handles food, every time.

What it's worth

  • To Sandals: the feeling of a personal butler, extended to every guest and every team, not only the suites that come with one.
  • To Royal Caribbean: on ships where crews rotate and a sailing carries thousands of guests, every crew member gets the memory of someone who has looked after that guest for years.
  • To both: fewer allergy scares, faster recovery when something went wrong last time, and a number the board can watch: recognition moments per stay, against satisfaction.
§8Beyond the gate

Every island, as the guest would want to see it

An all-inclusive can keep guests on property. The best trips don't stay there, and neither do the best port days.

Island Routes, Sandals' own excursion company, already runs tours across the Caribbean. Add the islands' own operators, kitchens, and guides through a partner portal that Sandals approves, and every place Sandals and the ships touch gets a living, approved map of what's worth doing.

  • One map, two kinds of guest. The same listing serves a resort guest with a free afternoon and a cruise guest with eight hours. Both see it scored against their own profile, with travel time and the all-aboard clock taken into account.
  • Partners own their data. Sandals approves what goes live. An edit never takes a live listing down, and a reviewer sees only what changed.
  • Structured answers, not adjectives. Partners describe intensity, group size, indoors or outdoors, and who it suits. That maps onto the same seven axes, so the recommender learns something real.
  • More of the island, per guest. The operators who fit a guest get found by that guest, and the money a trip brings reaches further than the resort gate and the pier.

§10Getting it right

What it takes, named up front

  1. Consent first, and visible. Nothing moves between brands without the guest's yes, and nothing moves between the companies before the deal closes and the right agreements are in place. The prototypes run on invented guests.
  2. Protect what makes Sandals, Sandals. Port-day features point guests to the island, not to the resort pool. Resorts stay for resort guests. The ship's job is to make people want to come back for a week.
  3. Keep advisors whole. Attribution for every booking the app inspires, and a view of the client the advisor can use. If advisors see the app as competition, it fails.
  4. A hospitality tool, never a surveillance tool. Guest Connect shows each role only what helps it serve, logs every view, and shows the guest every note. It is never used to score guests, or to score the staff who use it.
  5. One contact budget across every brand. Five brands that each want one guest's attention is the fastest way to be muted. The contact governor keeps a single budget per guest, shared by every brand.
  6. Integration is the real work. Loyalty, reservations, and ship and resort systems on both sides. Start thin: status recognition first, preferences next, the full profile last.
  7. Agree on success early. Guests who take both kinds of vacation, time between vacations, repeat rate, advisor bookings. Not downloads.
§11The proposal

Two working prototypes, to see how it could work

We propose to build two working prototypes: real software on real phones, with invented guests, so Sandals and Royal Caribbean can see and feel how this could work before deciding what to build.

The guest app
  • One Sandals guest and one Royal Caribbean guest, from the first booking to the next vacation.
  • The app changing shape at the resort, at sea, and in port, with a port day planned around all-aboard.
  • Between Trips suggesting the next vacation, on water or on land.
Guest Connect
  • The same guests' cards, as the gangway, the front desk, and the dining room see them.
  • Status, allergies, and the occasion carried from a Sandals stay onto a ship.
  • A note one team adds, arriving on the next team's card and in the guest's own app.

The two run side by side, so you can watch a guest do something in one and a team member see it in the other. Neither touches real guest data or either company's systems, so both can be built and shown before the deal closes. Then we decide together what to build first.
