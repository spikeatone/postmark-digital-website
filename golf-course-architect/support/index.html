What is Golf Course Architect?

Golf Course Architect is a design-and-operate tycoon sim for iOS. You buy a parcel of real-world land, route and shape a golf course on it — holes, greens, bunkers, water, irrigation — then build it, grow the turf in, and open it for play. From there you run it: green fees, pace of play, agronomy, staff and the books, all while the world's course rankings keep moving. You are the architect and the owner, never the golfer.

FAQ

What's free, and what needs Pro?

The whole game is playable for free — every design tool, the full economy, nothing trimmed down. The free tier lets you build one course, start to finish. Pro removes that limit so you can build an entire empire of courses.

How do I restore my purchase?

Open the app, bring up the Pro upgrade screen and tap Restore Purchases. Restores run through the Apple Account that made the original purchase, so make sure that account is the one signed in on the device.

Where are my saved courses?

There are three save slots, stored on your device. The app also keeps a copy in your own private iCloud storage, so a course can survive reinstalling the app or moving to a new device. We never receive your game data and there is no server behind the game, so we can't recover a save for you.

My course is built but nothing is happening. Why?

A new course doesn't earn anything until it opens. After you break ground it goes through construction and then grow-in — the turf has to establish before anyone can play it, which takes in-game time and costs money the whole way. That delay is the point: it's what makes your first course pay for your second. The Build tab shows the stage, the days remaining and the budget as it burns.

Why is my grow-in taking so long?

Three things stretch it. The grass you seeded — if it's a climate mismatch for that parcel, grow-in takes twice as long and the course never quite presents at its best, so watch for the warning on the seeding screen. The season you seeded in — spring and autumn establish fastest, mid-summer is slower and the off-season is slowest by far. And the program you chose, from minimal to accelerated. You can open early once the turf is far enough along, but a rushed opening leaves a lasting mark on the course's condition and rating.

What makes a course "good"?

Fitness to its category, not one absolute score. A short, wide, forgiving track is a triumph for a municipal course and a failure for a prestige one — the target moves. The Break Ground screen scores your routing against whichever category you pick and tells you, in plain language, what's helping and what isn't: hole crossings, long walks between green and next tee, length for that category, variety, and whether your amenities fit.

Can I get my course into the world rankings?

Yes — that's the long game. Your open courses are ranked against a world of rival courses on two lists: Top 100 You Can Play, which is the arena you can actually win, and the overall Top 100, which includes the private giants and is the rarer achievement. The rival world moves on its own, so a ranking has to be held, not just won.

Can I hire someone to run a course, or design one for me?

Both, and they cost you different things. A general manager takes a course off your hands — it runs fine, but it will never run great, so delegate the ordinary ones to buy attention for the special ones. Hiring an architect gets you a genuinely good course quickly, but it's their masterpiece, not yours: only courses you design yourself count toward earning access to prestige land.
